# Customer reports

- Small credits sometimes shortchange customers by one cent. Returning one of
  two units on a USD 1.01 charge records USD -0.50 instead of USD -0.51. Likewise,
  returning one of two USD 1.03 items bought with 10% off produces a credit
  note for USD -0.92 instead of USD -0.93 (the two items cost USD 1.85).
  A USD -1.01 credit converted at a 1/2 rate records only -0.50 instead of
  -0.51 in the billing currency. Positive half-cent charges round up as expected.
- Standard delivery to the US for a single 750 g item costing USD 20.00 is
  quoted at USD 754.00; it should be USD 5.00. A 1250 g parcel should cost
  USD 6.00 for standard delivery, but customers see USD 1254.00. No coupon or
  tax is involved in either example.
- Foreign-currency receipts disagree with the saved order and sales totals.
  For a digital USD 10.00 purchase billed in EUR at 9/10, the order and sales
  total correctly say EUR 9.00, but the receipt says EUR 8.10. At a GBP rate of
  4/5, the saved GBP 8.00 purchase is printed as GBP 6.40. USD receipts agree.
